Half of every phonics screening check is alien words: made-up words like jigh, phope and splote that a child can only read by sounding them out. Here are all 260 of them from the 13 published papers (2012 to 2026), grouped by year. A few come back in more than one year, so there are 257 different ones.

They make a good warm-up: read one year's twenty a night, and look for the letter team that slows your child down. For why they exist and how to practise them without worksheets, see Alien words, explained.

Questions parents ask

How many alien words are in the phonics screening check?

20 of the 40 words are alien words. They are words 1 to 12 and 21 to 28.

Will these exact alien words be in next year's check?

Very unlikely. Most words are new each year, and the check is designed so that memorising words gives no advantage. Use these lists to practise the sounds, not the words.

Why does the check use alien words?

Because a child can't have memorised a made-up word. Reading one shows they can turn letters into sounds and blend them, which is the skill the check is measuring.
