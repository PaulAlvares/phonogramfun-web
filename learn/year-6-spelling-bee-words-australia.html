Here are 45 spelling bee words for Year 6, in three levels: a warm-up round, middle rounds and final rounds. They work for a class bee, a school bee or a practice bee at the kitchen table, and they use Australian spelling (favourite, neighbour, jewellery) throughout.

By Year 6, spelling bees get competitive. The final-round words (acquiesce, conscientious, entrepreneur, liaison, millennium) are where they're won and lost, so practise those the most.

How a spelling bee works

  1. The caller says the word, uses it in a sentence, and says the word again.
  2. The speller may ask for the word to be repeated, used in another sentence, or defined.
  3. The speller says the word, spells it letter by letter, and says it again.
  4. A wrong letter is out for that round. Once a letter is said, it can't be taken back.

How to practise for a bee

Spell out loud, not on paper. A bee is spoken, and it's surprisingly different: children who write a word perfectly can lose their place saying it. Chunk long words into parts (acquiesce → acquiesce). Learn the story behind odd words: where a word comes from often explains its spelling. And finish every practice on a word they get right.

Questions parents ask

What words are in a Year 6 spelling bee?

There's no official list for most school bees in Australia; teachers choose words that suit the class. These 45 words cover the patterns Year 6 children learn, plus harder words for the final rounds.

How do you practise for a spelling bee?

Spell the words out loud rather than writing them, break long words into chunks, and spend most time on the hardest words. Finish each session on a word your child spells correctly.
